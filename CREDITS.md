# Credits and Acknowledgements

PcW16 BASIC is an independent, open-source project, but it builds on the work, generosity and curiosity of people who have kept the Amstrad PcW16 alive long after its commercial life ended.

When I returned to developing software for the PcW16 in 2026, I discovered that much of the information needed to understand this unusual machine was still available. That was only possible because people had taken the time to document, archive, share and preserve it.

I want to acknowledge some of those contributions.

## John Elliott

A particular thank you to John Elliott, creator of the JOYCE and ANNE emulators.

ANNE (which I use as XAnne on Linux) has been essential to developing and testing PcW16 BASIC. Being able to run Rosanne and investigate the behaviour of native applications on a modern computer has made this project practical.

John's extensive technical documentation, preservation work and contributions to understanding Amstrad's computers have also been invaluable.

## Rob Buckley

Thank you to Rob Buckley, who developed an earlier BASIC compiler for the PcW16 and kindly shared his work with me many years ago.

Rob's compiler, written using Visual Basic, demonstrated that it was possible to create BASIC programs for a machine that never received a mainstream BASIC development environment.

His work stayed with me and was a direct inspiration for developing this new compiler using Python and Z80 assembly.

PcW16 BASIC is a new, independent implementation, but Rob deserves recognition for helping spark the idea.

## The PcW16 preservation community

Thank you to everyone who preserved original PcW16 software, rescue disks, utilities, documentation, technical references, archived websites and discussions.

Some of these materials have survived for more than thirty years, often through the efforts of individuals maintaining personal websites, sharing files or keeping old development archives accessible.

The original Rosanne programming documentation, assembler include files and surviving technical discussions have been especially important in understanding how native applications interact with the operating system.

Not everyone who contributed to this preservation effort is known to me by name, but their work has made a real difference.

## The original developers

I also want to acknowledge the engineers and software developers at Amstrad and Creative Technology who designed the PcW16 hardware and developed Rosanne and its original applications.

Revisiting the machine's architecture and software has given me a renewed appreciation for what they achieved with a 16 MHz Z80, banked memory and the hardware available at the time.

## Keeping the machine alive

PcW16ers began in 1999 as a small community around an unusual computer that I had grown very fond of.

PcW16 BASIC continues that interest: not simply preserving what existed, but making it possible for people to create something new.

My hope is that the compiler, its source code and the documentation will help others explore the PcW16, learn about its architecture and develop applications of their own.

Thank you to everyone who helped make that possible.
